These notes go with `sheets/storyboard.jpg`, `sheets/situations.jpg` and `preview/storyboard.mp4`.

## 2. Linkages COVERAGE.md did not list

1. **A third colour can re-key the place.** Under 1.13 the half takes the *heaviest* pair. When Evan's Island
   arrived, island+plains (2.5 lands) outweighed mountain+plains (2.0). The whole half would swap from Plateau's
   mesas to Tundra's coast at turn 8, with no front to cover it. Row 6 treats the third colour as an addition, but
   in practice it can replace the place.
2. **One road serves three rows.** The fetch's road into the mist (rows 8 and 13) becomes the dual's road up the
   seam (row 5, the pairs-v3.1 motifs). One motif family covers the prologue, the fetch and the dual.
3. **A dual arrives without a front.** A fetch cracked for a dual, or a dual played first, lands two colours at
   once, so row 12's fronts never fire for it. Fronts are for basics; the road opening is the dual's arrival. The
   front set (30 frames) needs a "both at once" case, or the road takes it.
4. **The game arc never reaches "late" in a normal game.** C2's thresholds put late at round 9+. This game ended
   in round 7, so its warm grade would never have shown. (The storyboard uses 6+ so the beat can be seen.) The arc
   should key on something every game reaches:
   - the table's highest stage, which C1 already reads;
   - or life lost;
   - not the round number.
5. **≤ 5 misses the game's biggest moment.** 15 → 6 in one turn drew nothing. Options, all plain reads of the
   life total and no rules logic:
   - a threshold in proportion to starting life (≤ 30%, which also scales to 30- or 40-life formats);
   - plus a one-turn squall on a big single loss (≥ a third of starting life).
6. **The late grade and the top stages double up.** Stage-3 pair pictures are already dusk or red-sky. A warm
   grade on top over-saturates (B09, B10), and a winner's sunrise on top reads as haze (B11). Grades should be
   relative to each picture's own hour, which `light.json` already records.
7. **The far half's subject sits under its own cards.** With card rows at roughly a quarter of the half's height
   each, the opponent's lands cover their sky and horizon (the top of their half). What stays visible is their
   foreground, right at the mist. The far country is mostly read through its ground and colour, not its skyline,
   so far-country haze matters more than far-country detail.
8. **Stacked halves put your sky against their ground.** At the centre line the near half's sky meets the far
   half's foreground: two postcards, with mist between. C7's "one view, near and far" needs more than haze. It
   needs a rule about which half keeps its sky (decision 1).
9. **Mirroring a shared pair.** When both players share a pair, the far half shows the same picture. Mirrored
   (the prologue does this) it reads as repetition; unmirrored it reads as a copy. The far country may want a
   different pan or stage of the same place.
10. **Weather and the scrim compete for the same band.** Fronts, storms, dust and inhabitants all live in the
    subject band, where the creature row sits. Spec 1.4 allows one full-area piece per stage, so these need the
    additive overlay field COVERAGE already flagged. They also need an order: storm over front over element.

## 3. A solid footing: four decisions before anything is painted properly

1. **One view or two postcards?**
   - Either the two halves stay two upright pictures with a mist band (today), or the board becomes one view
     across a valley: the far half keeps the sky and horizon, and the near half is mostly ground, its sky given
     up to the mist.
   - This sets every picture's crop and horizon (the cloud's shared-horizon work) and whether the far half
     mirrors.
2. **What owns time?**
   - The proposal: the stage keeps the painting's hour. The game adds a grade of at most a third, relative to
     each picture's hour, keyed to the table's highest stage or life lost, not to rounds (§2.4, §2.6).
   - The end either holds the top stage's light (cheap) or breaks the ladder with a painted clearing for the
     winner (about 15 pictures).
3. **What is a place?**
   - Either the first pair names the half and later colours are elements (stable, B08), or the heaviest pair
     re-keys the place (1.13 today, §2.1).
   - The same answer decides triples: element pieces (row 6, about 2 GPU-h), or painted triples (6b, about
     23 GPU-h).
4. **How loud is the game?**
   - Which events the land itself shows, and at what thresholds: the storm (≤ 5, or ≤ 30% plus a squall), the
     fronts, the combat band, the end.
   - What stays in the UI instead (whose turn, the stack, inhabitants).
   - The sketches suggest weather (fronts, storms, the end) is loud and legible, and small marks (+5% light,
     dust, herds) are not worth their cost.

Once these four are answered, COVERAGE's batch 3 can be re-cut against them. The fronts, the prologue, the peril
veils and the end are the beats this storyboard shows working.

## 4. Settled (2026-10-22)

Justin found §3's questions confusing. The PC translated them, he did not object, and the translation is now the
default. **`../SYSTEM.md` is the spec**, and its decision list (§A) and slot table (§B) are what later batches fill.

1. **One view or two postcards → two landscapes.** Each player's land situation gets its own picture. The two are
   stacked, and their light is matched by far-half haze plus a capped pull toward one table hour (SYSTEM §A9). They
   are never one continuous valley.
2. **What owns time → the land pictures.** The light stays painted into each stage. The turn/life grade, the storm
   and the end's sunrise are optional later layers, outside the system (SYSTEM §C).
3. **What is a place → the combination on the board now.**
   - The heaviest pair names the base picture, and it re-keys when that pair changes. Evan's Plateau → Tundra at T8
     is therefore the intended behaviour.
   - A third colour is an element in the picture. Triples are elements, not paintings.
   - A transition plays whenever the makeup changes.
   - A unique land is a landmark on top: the spirit of the card's art, never the art. A fetch or utility land changes
     nothing.
4. **How loud is the game → quiet.** Only the land system is in. Every other event (the storm, combat, the turn,
   the stack, inhabitants) is parked by name in SYSTEM §C.
